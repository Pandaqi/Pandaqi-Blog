_In an attempt to play more games as a game developer and learn from them, I've started trying out all sorts of games. This week I've tried "Balatro". Below are my quick thoughts on it!_

Ah yes, Balatro. The mega super hit of 2024. Which led to post after post from people saying they were inspired by it and making a "Balatro-like game" in the game development spaces all throughout 2025. It was amusing to follow this trend from a distance, but I never actually played the game and thus had no idea what they were actually talking about. All I knew about the game was from an interview with the developer that I read, and the screenshots showing it had something to do with jokers and poker.

My interest was piqued. Something that inspires so many people should be worth something. When I made my list of games to try out, Balatro was pretty close to the top.

Now I finally got to play it! So, what do I think? And what did I learn from it?

## A Great Game ...

Yes, it's a great game. It taught itself in a very efficient and simple way when I booted the game. Before long, I was playing and no more tutorial was needed, and I kept playing for a while.

Admittedly, I've played a lot of card games with standard deck, including poker, in my life. I did not have to look up what a Flush or a Straight was, or what their implicit order in the ranking was. Someone without that knowledge will surely struggle a bit more here.

The sparse tutorial obviously has some downsides too. It took until my third attempt before I realized I wasn't _forced_ to play 5 cards for each hand :p Don't know why I assumed that and never tried something else. (Poker is about creating the "best combination" out of 5 cards, I guess that's why I assumed it would be the same.) But it wasn't until I hit some more weird Jokers and Boss powers that I suddenly realized I could just play fewer cards in a hand.

For the most part, though, I did not struggle to understand anything and was just _playing_ and _trying stuff_ rather quickly.

And then deck-building is simply a very effective and addictive game loop. To not only use certain tools to reach a highscore, but also the ability to "design" your tools to be better and better, is a positive feedback loop that will always work in a game. 

The idea to split your points into "base points" and "multiplier" is also the kind of design decision that seems small (or maybe obvious to some), but could probably have entire books written about it. In fact, when I saw people making their own "Balatro-clone", they usually meant copying the _multiplier mechanic_. They didn't copy the deck, or the poker hands, or any of the other stuff. They copied the multiplier scoring + being able to build/upgrade your bag of tools, whatever they are.

It inspired my brain to immediately come up with a few game ideas with that same core, simply applied to a different theme and structure around it. It's quite easy to code/prototype, but I've experienced now how easily it creates satisfying gameplay.

Another strength of such a design is "randomness mitigation". My biggest gripe with poker is the massive luck factor and high chance of having a hand worth _absolutely nothing_. (Yes, I know there's bluffing and strategic folding and all, but in a casual family environment without real stakes all of that means nothing and you're just left with a bad game.) But the fact you're upgrading all your tools (deck + hands + discards) all the time gives you a way to overcome that. It makes you feel like bad hands or bad luck is just because you didn't buy the right upgrades or playing cards. It makes you want to learn and play better next time, instead of being annoyed at the randomness.

I think Balatro is not just an example of a strong core, but also a strong execution. There are so many little details in exactly how the game flows, how the shop works, how the jokers are constructed, that elevate the game. If those details hadn't been there, or if the developer had made a different decision, the game would likely be _less_ than the sum of its parts. 

For example, the "small blind -> big blind -> boss blind" structure. Why is it here? Why can you skip the blinds? Why not just have "levels" or "bosses" or a map like Slay the Spirte that you move through? Why _this particular structure_ that seems needlessly complicated at first? Probably because it was the most fun. Because it fits the theme and works really well.

Similarly, why is the shop cut into three sections, with some having only one option and some two? Why do you get "interest" over money you already had at the start of a round? It's all these tiny decisions to give parts of the game more detail, more nuance, more specific rules that elevate it beyond just "make number go up". 

Or, stated another way, I think this is a part of game design I'm lacking right now. I tend to take the most simple, minimalist, straight route towards my destination. (Shop? Let me just list all things you can buy with a big button next to them. That's a shop! Functional! Done!) 

And ... it creates games that just feel mediocre, that feel like they could've been more, that feel a bit dull and lifeless. The idiosyncracies of the _execution_ of this idea are what make Balatro feel really good and unique to play. (Even to someone who hates poker!) I feel like many of the "clones" fail to capture that and therefore are less successful.

## ... That Somehow Became Less Interesting Over Time

So, I'm playing this game. On my first attempt, I get to Ante 4 _easily_ ... and then fail the big boss _horribly_. (As in: I had such bad luck with cards that I only score High Cards and one Pair.) I discover some parts of the game I didn't know yet. I learn some lessons and prevent this from happening again.

I play again and get to Ante 5 _easily_. Never any trouble. Barely need to think about what I do as I just stumble into loads of points. And then I fail the big boss again.

I start doing runs where I focus on just one, maybe two, specific poker hands. Such as investing in Straights, or Flushes, or Two Pair, etcetera. Get the Jokers to support it, upgrade the level through Planets, get a relevant Voucher when needed. This gets me to Ante 6 _easily_, actually getting bored by how easy it is, until I horribly fail the big boss again.

I'm still excited though. There are all these different things to try! Go for different hands. Focus more or less on discards, play more or fewer cards in a hand. When buying more cards, do I focus on high-value ones or those that make my chosen poker hands more likely?

This repeats a handful of times. The game starts so easy that I feel like any move works and it's not that interesting---but then it _suddenly_ ramps up into some difficulty that feels impossible to overcome.

Over time, I realize my creative approaches are just not going to work. This game is about the Jokers. Get lucky, get the right ones, and then just play the simplest hands (High Card, One Pair). Actual poker hands, actually designing your run (from the start/with more intent), it's just not the way to go if you want to get further. 

{{% remark %}}
After some research, I did learn about decks, jokers, etcetera that only unlock once you're further into the game and that allow these more varied playstyles a bit more. But they're obviously not going to help me at the start.
{{% /remark %}}

So, when I abandon the thing that _I thought made the game fun_, that's when I actually get further and this weird difficulty spike disappears.

Now I'm a bit torn.

I still think it's a great game. It makes sense that it focused more on the Jokers and all the crazy effects you can put on cards, and I can enjoy playing with that too. But it's less interesting than how I played the game before. I need to _beat_ those Bosses to get further, but to do so I have to ... have less fun playing?

Or look at it another way. When it comes to Jokers, I'm beholden to the random ones that spawn and whether they work with whatever I'm doing. (If I go for a certain poker hand, but I just never get cards that enhance it, now my run is sure to fail.) As I play more, I can memorize more of the Jokers and which ones work together well, but that's where it ends.

When it comes to making the best poker hands/combinations, though, the gameplay is much _deeper_ and more _varied_. You always have the same known deck of cards. Your hand is always different, forcing you to adapt and build an intuition for the best way to play that hand. There is no memorizing or getting lucky, each run is about growing more skilled and getting a deeper understanding of how to get slightly better at the game.

Each "hand" is about looking at some completely unique arrangement of cards and trying to squeeze the absolute best cleverest combination out of it (that also sets up the best combination for _next_ turn). That would engage my brain completely and feel most satisfying. (It would also likely require being able to look ahead for your next draws or design your deck even more.) That would be _different_ each time, requiring improvision and intuition over just memorization. A more rewarding puzzle/challenge that you get for free by using a deck and poker combinations.

I like depth and variety more than memorization and randomness. That's why I would've liked the game to be way more focused on your cards and combinations, and for it to be easily possible to win based on just that. The Jokers, Tarot cards, enhancing cards---it would all just be a _bonus_, an extra fun system _on top_, that you'd need to beat the _hardest bosses_. It would be a thing you start introducing as you get further.

This way, the start of the game would also be at a proper difficulty. Instead of it being boring at first and then suddenly spiking to impossible---at least in my experience---at which point you just pray that you happened to collect the right set of Jokers along the way.

## Conclusion

Balatro is a great game. Quick to get into, a nice flow to every run you play, lots of choices and crazy Jokers and satisfying numbers going up. 

At the same time, for me personally, the _focus_ of the game---and how you actually score crazy high numbers and progress/win---is not where I would have wanted it to be. Trying to beat it now felt more like a mix of "studying" the game and just executing that one strategy ad nauseum without thinking. (Combined with a bit of luck, but that's to be expected in a deck-building game.) The better I played in terms of numbers and level/ante, the less fun I was having.

I wanted the incredible depth that a card combination mechanic can bring. I like games that teach a kind of skill or _intuition_ almost with every play. Basically, games that are close to training for sports, getting just a little faster or more precise with every exercise or match you do. Instead, the more I played, the more I just learned the best Jokers, stopped going for any hands other than the basic ones, and memorized the counter-that-always-works to a Boss's tricks.

{{% remark %}}
Granted, I haven't played the game for hundreds of hours and are not very skilled. Maybe my assessment is completely wrong once you get very skilled/experienced and stumble into the incredible depth of the poker combinations in this game.

But that's exactly why I believe it's an issue to easily breeze through Ante 1--6 in my first games and then suddenly hitting a wall. It makes more sense if a newbie can beat only the first two levels and then hits a _minor_ wall. They get better, they get more experience, now they can beat the first three levels and hit a slightly bigger wall. It feels weird to have _no clue_ what you're doing and still waltz through large parts of the game.

Also, I've done research and discovered my experience isn't unique. Most new players report starting by trying to get smart combinations, never win, then look up some help or guide that tells them to forget about the cards and just go for the best Jokers.
{{% /remark %}}

Could that be fixed? Is that an issue with the game? I don't know. 

Lots of people love those other aspects and therefore love the game (and are much better at it than I am!) Some people report never ever beating the game, because they're just experimenting with silly plays and Jokers and clever "poker hands", having fun doing that. Basically, the same thing that _I_ thought was fun about this game, but also proving that this won't really bring you wins/progress.

After giving it some thought, I think it's mostly a "me" thing. The game _does_ still allow for varying strategies and gaining some deeper knowledge. Whenever I've had to wait for 15--30 minutes, or take a break, I've been booting up the game because it's still fun and so easy to just get started. I guess my hyperactive brain just wants really deep gameplay and lots of intuitive skill as I build experience with a game, and, well, it's pretty darn hard to design a game for that. It's why I've always played sports (and board games actually) more than video games.

Through some combination of skill (built through experience) and simply repeating the "optimal" steps (boring they may be), I will probably beat a large part of the game soon. After hitting that milestone I will likely call it quits and move on to the next game. Not because I didn't enjoy my time or think it's a bad game, but because what it focuses on didn't match with what my brain wants the most.

Those were my thoughts,
