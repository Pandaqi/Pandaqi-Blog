_In an attempt to play more games as a game developer and learn from them, I've started trying out all sorts of games. This week I've tried "Risk: Global Domination". Below are my quick thoughts on it!_

I am more a boardgamer than a videogamer actually. That's why I added some video game versions of board games into my long list of varied games to try! This game is pretty popular, easy to download/play, and most of all _free_! I gave it a try.

I enjoyed myself for a few hours, but it didn't really stick with me.

Like many people, I played a lot of Risk as a kid. And I always felt that there was a great game lurking underneath the surface of minor annoyances and unlucky dice rolls. 

This game ... well ... it's just Risk again. So it has many of the same flaws. Or, rather, it stays true to the original a bit _too much_.

A digital version of Risk has the opportunity to evolve the idea in a way that the boardgame can't. It can make things happen automatically. It can use settings to turn stuff on/off. It can introduce quality of life features.

For the most part, _Risk: Global Domination_ does this well. The game looks good and is very responsive. (It's actually _really hard_ to create a game that both looks good and is easily readable at a glance. Much harder than just going for "abstract" or "photorealistic".) It has some nice tutorial missions to teach the game, in which it doesn't actually teach _all of it_---because some parts are handled automatically by the computer anyway.

Where it really shines is _custom maps_ and _custom settings_. As a kid I drew my own Risk maps, though I wasn't smart enough to actually make them playable. (I basically just drew fantasy maps of nonexistent lands cut into random zones. And at a scale that's far too small to play Risk on :p) It's such an obvious way to give the game longevity and to really make each one feel different. 

The settings they implemented in R:GD also make a lot of sense. 

* The blizzards permanently disable/deactivate several regions of the map. This even allows you to play the same map over and over while needing a completely different strategy each time.
* The fog means you can only see what's next to your troops, making it more important to forge alliances or spend a turn or two just exploring the board.
* The capitals mean you get a specific area to protect and call your home (which is safer too), but also creates new ways for weaker players to still win (by sneakily taking capitals)
* The "exponential" setting means players get more troops for trading cards as the game goes on, guaranteeing it comes to an end more quickly than otherwise.

I would actually like to see even more creative improvements here. There are waaaaay more things one can imagine to simplify the game, shake it up, remove the busywork. But what they've done with the game is already pretty great.

Busywork? Improve the game? Staying "too true" to the original? What are you talking about!? 

Yes, good point imaginary reader, let's get more specific now.

* Battles are still done through dice rolls. You can have a massive advantage and still lose. I think I speak for many people when I say that I want to lose a game because the other player was smarter and better, not because of random bad luck all the time.
* Battles are still one territory at a time and turns have a bit too many steps. It's a lot of clicking, clicking again, doing the same thing again, then the opponent takes it back, then I do it again. If someone has 20 territories remaining with 1 troop on each, I am certain I will win the game ... but I still need to go through all the stupid effort of clicking on each and every one several times in the right order.
* Similar to the dice, you still get those _cards_ for claiming territories (showing Infantry, Cavalry, or Artillery), and your _rewards_ for trading them are thus pretty random. In the games I've played, and the ones I watched on YouTube afterwards, it happened way too often that bad luck with these cards basically _decided the whole game_ (at the end). That's not fun or interesting---it makes the 80% of the game that came before feel meaningless.

I know that these things are in the original boardgame. And I am not against randomness or some luck factor in games (more on that soon). But this is just too fiddly and too luck-based, and there was no reason to bring all of that into the video game.

* You could give players some tools to perform "busywork" automatically. (Or have a setting or creative twist to games that removes it, which people can turn on or off.) For a very crude example, just be able grab a stack and tell it to attack Red's territories until it can't anymore.
* You could remove any details from those cards and just make it "3 cards trades for 3X troops", "4 cards trades for 5X troops", etc. This would reward people for holding out on trading, making it skill-based instead of luck-based.
  * If you want to keep the territories on the cards, which play a small role too (though the tutorial never explains that), then you can remove the cards entirely and JUST let people "collect territories". If you collect all territories from one continent, for example, you get that bonus. A simple rule that makes it skill-based.
* The game adds the "blitz" mechanic to make the dice rolling a bit more involved, but it's too weak if you ask me. Again, numerous games lost or won because of insane bad luck on an attack someone by all means should have won---they earned it through good play. I don't see why the dice rolling is there at all. A video game can replace it with something both simpler and better for gameplay.

Now, as a game designer, I know why randomness exists in games. I have _seen_ it time and time again. Thanks to Risk's randomness, weak players can make miraculous comebacks, and superior players can face setbacks and suddenly anyone can win again. It allows both skilled and less skilled players to step into the same arena and have odds that are at least a little more even.

I just think the randomness is _too_ big in Risk, and this video game adaptation does too little to soften that. For a game that's _all about_ taking territories and getting better numbers in better places, it feels silly to have so much of that core be decided by random dice rolls. 

You could imagine a number of mechanics that both keep _some_ randomness but otherwise make the game more strategical and tactically interesting. For example, 

* Different _terrains_ for fighting. A 50-troop army on bad terrain can still lose to a 20-troop army positioned on ideal terrain.
* Having some more permanent properties, such as _fatigue_. More fatigued soldiers _create_ more randomness and more chance of losing against inferior armies.
* Or you can reuse existing elements like the incredibly important "connections" between zones. In regular battles, it's just a 1-to-1 troop loss, but on "crucial battles" (like connections or someone's capital) it changes to something with more randomness.
* Or you can put some basic caps on the randomness, like "if your army is more than twice as big you always win"

Just throwing some ideas out there. 

As it stands, Risk: Global Domination is probably the _best possible digital adaptation of Risk_ one could create. But Risk itself is too flawed at its core to be truly interesting to me. After losing another game due to bad spawn + die rolls + card draws, you feel like your effort is wasted and don't get any fun out of trying to overcome that challenge. We're not talking about fine margins here. We're talking about battles where I lose 30 troops and the other magically only loses 5 or so.
